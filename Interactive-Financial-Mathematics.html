Sequences, Series, Simple Interest, Compound Interest & Annuities

1. Arithmetic Sequences & Series

Arithmetic Sequence (n-th Term)
an = a1 + (n − 1)d

When to use: Calculate a specific term in a sequence that increases or decreases by a constant common difference (d) at equal steps.

Result: a6 = 7,500.00
Real-World Example:
A worker starts with a monthly salary of ₱5,000 (a1 = 5,000) and receives an annual fixed step increment of ₱500 (d = 500). Salary in Year 6 (n = 6):
a6 = 5,000 + (6 − 1)(500) = ₱7,500.00
Sum of a Finite Arithmetic Series
Sn = [ n (a1 + an) ] / 2   or   Sn = [ n (2a1 + (n − 1)d) ] / 2

When to use: Find the total cumulative sum of all terms in an arithmetic sequence across n periods.

Result: S6 = 37,500.00
Real-World Example:
Total cumulative earnings over 6 years with initial ₱5,000 salary and ₱500 annual increment:
S6 = [ 6 × (2(5,000) + (6 − 1)(500)) ] / 2 = ₱37,500.00

2. Geometric Sequences & Series

Geometric Sequence (n-th Term)
an = a1 · rn−1

When to use: Calculate a specific single term in a sequence that grows or decays by a constant multiplier ratio (r).

Result: a5 = 12,155.06
Real-World Example:
A company starts production at 10,000 units in Year 1 (a1 = 10,000) with a 5% yearly increase (r = 1.05). Target output for Year 5 (n = 5):
a5 = 10,000 × (1.05)4 = 12,155.06 units
Sum of a Finite Geometric Series
Sn = [ a1 (1 − rn) ] / (1 − r)

When to use: Compute the cumulative sum of all terms in a geometric sequence from period 1 to n.

Result: S5 = 55,256.31
Real-World Example:
Total cumulative production across all 5 years combined (a1 = 10,000, r = 1.05):
S5 = [ 10,000 × (1 − 1.055) ] / (1 − 1.05) = 55,256.31 units

3. Simple Interest & Future Value

Simple Interest (Interest & Future Value)
I = P · r · t   |   FV = P(1 + r · t)

When to use: Calculate linear interest (I) or total Future Value (FV) charged/earned strictly on the original principal balance (P) over time (t in years).

Interest: ₱3,000.00 | Future Value: ₱23,000.00
Real-World Example:
Depositing ₱20,000 (P = 20,000) at 5% simple interest per year (r = 0.05) for 3 years (t = 3):
FV = 20,000 × (1 + 0.05 × 3) = ₱23,000.00

4. Compound Interest & Future Value

Compound Interest (Future Value of a Single Sum)
FV = P (1 + i)n

When to use: Find the total Future Value (FV) of a single initial deposit (P) left to compound exponentially over n periods at periodic rate i.

Future Value: ₱63,123.85
Real-World Example:
Depositing ₱50,000 (P = 50,000) at 6% interest compounded annually (i = 0.06) for 4 years (n = 4):
FV = 50,000 × (1.06)4 = ₱63,123.85

5. Annuities & Loans

Periodic Payment for Loans & Mortgages (Amortization)
R = P · [ i / (1 − (1 + i)−n) ]

When to use: Calculate the regular equal payment R needed to amortize a loan principal P over n periods.

Monthly Payment: ₱8,438.57
Real-World Example:
A ₱1,000,000 mortgage (P = 1,000,000) at 6% annual rate compounded monthly (i = 0.005) over 15 years (n = 180):
R = 1,000,000 × [ 0.005 / (1 − (1.005)−180) ] = ₱8,438.57 / month
Future Value of an Ordinary Annuity
FV = P · [ ((1 + i)n − 1) / i ]

When to use: Find total accumulated wealth from regular periodic deposits P over n compounding periods.

Future Value: ₱108,537.27
Real-World Example:
Saving ₱2,000 quarterly (P = 2,000) at 6% per annum compounded quarterly (i = 0.015) for 10 years (n = 40):
FV = 2,000 × [ ((1.015)40 − 1) / 0.015 ] = ₱108,537.27

Unified Concept Integration

Financial mathematics spans linear and exponential models derived directly from fundamental sequences:

  • Arithmetic Growth & Simple Interest: Model linear growth where additions occur via a constant additive difference (d = P · r).
  • Geometric Growth & Compound Interest: Model exponential compounding where balances scale by a constant common ratio (r = 1 + i).
  • Annuities & Amortization: Aggregate multiple geometric payments or present values into unified series sums.

1. Arithmetic Sequences & Series

Arithmetic Sequence (n-th Term)
an = a1 + (n − 1)d

When to use: Calculate a specific term in a sequence that increases or decreases by a constant common difference (d).

Result: a6 = 7,500.00
Real-World Example:
A worker starts with a monthly salary of ₱5,000 (a1 = 5,000) and receives an annual increment of ₱500 (d = 500). Salary in Year 6 (n = 6):
a6 = 5,000 + (6 − 1)(500) = ₱7,500.00
Sum of a Finite Arithmetic Series
Sn = [ n (a1 + an) ] / 2   |   Sn = [ n (2a1 + (n − 1)d) ] / 2

When to use: Find the total cumulative sum of all terms across n periods.

Result: S6 = 37,500.00
Real-World Example:
Total cumulative earnings over 6 years with initial ₱5,000 salary and ₱500 annual increment:
S6 = [ 6 × (2(5,000) + (6 − 1)(500)) ] / 2 = ₱37,500.00

2. Geometric Sequences & Series

Geometric Sequence (n-th Term)
an = a1 · rn−1

When to use: Calculate a specific single term in a sequence that grows or decays by a multiplier ratio (r).

Result: a5 = 12,155.06
Real-World Example:
Production starts at 10,000 units (a1 = 10,000) and increases by 5% yearly (r = 1.05). Output for Year 5 (n = 5):
a5 = 10,000 × (1.05)4 = 12,155.06 units
Sum of a Finite Geometric Series
Sn = [ a1 (1 − rn) ] / (1 − r)

When to use: Compute the cumulative sum of all terms in a geometric sequence from period 1 to n.

Result: S5 = 55,256.31
Real-World Example:
Total cumulative production across all 5 years combined:
S5 = [ 10,000 × (1 − 1.055) ] / (1 − 1.05) = 55,256.31 units

3. Simple Interest & Derived Unknowns

Simple Interest: Solving for I, P, r, or t
I = P · r · t   |   P = I / (r · t)   |   r = I / (P · t)   |   t = I / (P · r)

When to use: Solve for any single missing variable in a simple interest transaction.

Result: I = ₱3,000.00
Real-World Example (Solving for Rate r):
If ₱20,000 principal earns ₱3,000 interest in 3 years, what was the rate?
r = 3,000 / (20,000 × 3) = 0.05 = 5.00% per year

4. Compound Interest & Derived Unknowns

Compound Interest: Solving for FV, P, i, or n
FV = P(1+i)n  |  P = FV / (1+i)n  |  i = (FV/P)1/n − 1  |  n = ln(FV/P) / ln(1+i)

When to use: Calculate future value, initial deposit, periodic rate, or compounding periods for a single lump sum.

Result: FV = ₱63,123.85
Real-World Example (Solving for Periods n):
How long will ₱50,000 take to grow to ₱63,123.85 at 6% annual rate (i = 0.06)?
n = ln(63,123.85 / 50,000) / ln(1 + 0.06) = 4 periods (years)

5. Annuities, Loans & Frequency Modes

Ordinary Annuity & Loan Amortization
FV = R · [ ((1+i)n − 1) / i ]   |   PV = R · [ (1 − (1+i)−n) / i ]

When to use: Evaluate future accumulation ($FV$) or present loan value ($PV$) based on payment modes (Monthly, Quarterly, Semi-Annually, Annually).

FV: ₱108,537.27 | PV: ₱59,777.20
Key Conversions for Payment Modes:
  • i (Periodic Rate) = Annual Rate / Mode Frequency (i = r / m)
  • n (Total Payments) = Years × Mode Frequency (n = t · m)
Quarterly Example: r = 6%, m = 4, Years = 10 ➔ i = 0.06/4 = 0.015, n = 10×4 = 40

Unified Concept Integration

Financial mathematics models linear and exponential growth through foundational algebraic concepts:

  • Arithmetic Sequences & Simple Interest: Additive linear growth driven by constant difference steps (d = P · r).
  • Geometric Sequences & Compound Interest: Multiplicative exponential growth scaling by a common ratio (r = 1 + i).
  • Annuities & Amortization: Structured finite series combining periodic cash flows with exponential discounting or accumulation.
